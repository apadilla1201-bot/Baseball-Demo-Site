The method

Three steps. The same order every time.

Players tell us they plateau because every lesson changes something new. Here, nothing changes until the last change is proven.

Step oneAssess

Bat speed, exit velocity, spin rate and arm path. We record your baseline before anything is touched.

Step twoBuild

One change per block. You leave with one cue, one drill and one number to beat.

Step threeRepeat

We retest the same way. If the number moved and held, we move on. If it did not, we change the cue, not the player.

What I measure

Six numbers. Nothing vague.

Each one has a unit, a reason, and a retest date.

  • Bat speedmphShows whether the swing is getting faster or only looking different.
  • Exit velocitymphThe result that matters. How hard the ball leaves the bat.
  • Spin raterpmTells a pitcher whether the seam is doing its job.
  • Release heightinCatches arm slot drift before it becomes a habit.
  • Hip-shoulder separationdegThe source of most of the power. We track it on video.
  • 60-yard sprintsecA simple check on whether the legs are keeping up.

Questions parents ask

Straight answers.

Is one hour enough?

Yes, when it is planned. Ten minutes of warm-up and arm care, forty minutes on one change, ten minutes of testing.

Will you rebuild my player's swing?

No. I change one thing at a time, and only when the numbers show why it needs changing.

Can parents watch?

Yes. Stand wherever you like. I ask that you leave the coaching to me, so your player hears one voice.

What does it cost?

$95 per session, or five for $450. The assessment is $60 and counts toward your first pack of five.

Where do sessions happen?

In a private indoor lab with two hitting lanes and a pitching tunnel. The address comes with your booking.

My pitcher has arm pain. Can you help?

I am not a medical provider. Please see a doctor first. I will build the plan around their clearance.
